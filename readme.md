# [Positioning] Dual-Baseline ISAC Estimates of Joint Arrival and Departure Angles Based on Recurrent Neural Networks [with MATLAB-Python Code]

Wireless systems' Integrated Sensors and Communications (ISAC) have emerged as a promising paradigm, expected to enhance performance, achieve efficient resource utilization, and foster mutually beneficial interactions between radar sensors and wireless communications, shaping the future of wireless technology. In this work, we propose two novel methods to address the joint angle of arrival (AoA) and departure angle (AoD) estimation in dual-base station ISAC systems. Our proposed methods include a deep learning (DL) solution utilizing complex neural networks, and a parameterized algorithm. By leveraging the estimated channel matrix and incorporating preprocessing steps that include coarse timing estimation, we are able to significantly reduce the input size and improve computational efficiency. The research results highlight the significant potential of our DL-based approach, which exhibits comparable performance to explicitly parameterized methods that make use of multi-input multi-output (MIMO) models while significantly reducing computational complexity.
The emergence of Integrated Sensors and Communications (ISAC) signifies a transformative breakthrough in the 6G network domain [1]. While radar sensors and wireless communications traditionally developed independently, the rise of integrated communication and sensing has introduced a disruptive paradigm, offering unprecedented opportunities for improving spectral efficiency, reducing hardware costs and power consumption, and redefining ways of network perception and interacting with the surrounding environment. Against this backdrop, ISAC has attracted significant research interest and attention from both academic and industrial communities. Indeed, various scenarios and challenges have been explored, including dual-function radar and communication (DFRC) [2], unmanned aerial vehicles [3], waveform design [4], beamforming design [5], security [6], [7], and intelligent reflectors [8], [9].
The ISAC (Intelligent Sensor Array) centered around communication, which utilizes optimized communication waveforms for sensing tasks, represents a promising research area. By leveraging the transmitted communication signals to enhance sensing capabilities, substantial achievements are expected. The reuse of the channel state information estimation process offers an opportunity to accurately determine physical sensing parameters such as the arrival direction (DoA), distance, and velocity of interest targets. Indeed, numerous studies have examined and verified the integration of dual-baseline radar topologies with OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) signal processing to address the optimization issues of communication-aided sensing [10]-[12]. Notably, they explored this approach in actual dual-baseline settings [13], demonstrating its effectiveness in accurately detecting and estimating target parameters such as delay and Doppler shift. Additionally, [14] conducted a power budget analysis to study the behavior of communication and radar signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) as a function of distance. However, achieving high sensing quality from received communication signals remains challenging in practice, as not all communication and radar systems are optimized for sensing purposes. Designing radar signal processing functions presents various challenges, including peak-to-average power ratios [15], clutter noise, multipath reflections, unoptimized sidelobes, and Doppler ambiguity caused by cyclic prefixes (CP) [16].
In this work, we investigated the parameter estimation of dual-baseline radar settings in the ISAC system, focusing on estimating the Angle of Arrival (AoA) and Angle of Departure (AoD). Since maximum likelihood estimators are highly multidimensional optimization problems, it is not feasible to implement them in real-time using actual hardware resources. We proposed utilizing neural networks (NNs) as generalized approximations by employing low-complexity optimization NN architectures to perform this complex estimation problem. In literature [17], the AoA and AoD estimation problems were either constructed as regression problems [18], [19] or minimized the mean square error (MSE) objective function through training multilayer perceptron (MLP) architectures, or were constructed as spectral-based sensor estimation [20], [21] where NN was trained on a hot-coded ground truth vector representing the likelihood of the target at a given angle with a given baseband, without relying on prior knowledge of the number of targets. In the latter case, the network does not depend on prior knowledge of the number of targets. Instead, it is trained to output estimated spectrum, which then requires additional target detection steps to extract angle estimates from peaks, as is typically done in traditional methods like MUSIC. Other DL methods have been proposed, such as multi-stage DL [22] consisting of a multitask autoencoder and a series of parallel multi-layer classifiers. In our study, we proposed a novel method to solve the sensor estimation problem by leveraging unique inputs and training a complex value NN to minimize the MSE objective function on the training dataset generated using an ideal system model. Furthermore, we proposed a parametrized algorithm that leverages model knowledge for benchmark testing. Specifically, the parametrized 2D method utilizes complete knowledge of array steering vectors and OFDM subcarrier rule structures to estimate the AoA and AoD of the target. Although the model assumes OFDM usage, our work remains effective for any pre-coded OFDM modulation (e.g., Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) extended OFDM). For comparison, the Cramer-Rao bound (CRB) was also shown. The results indicate that DL-based solutions provide comparable estimation performance to parametrized methods while requiring significantly less computational effort.
Article Illustrations
Conclusion: This paper proposes two methods for joint AoA and AoD estimation in a dual-baseline ISAC system. One solution is based on deep learning (DL) methods, which utilize multivalued neural networks and incorporate preprocessing steps that include coarse timing estimation to reduce input size and improve computational efficiency. The second method employs a parameterized solution using model knowledge as a benchmark. Compared to the parameterized approach, the DL-based method demonstrates competitive performance while requiring lower online computational complexity for addition and multiplication operations. Moreover, the DL algorithm can accurately estimate the number of targets arriving at the same discrete time delay, making it suitable for dynamic environments. Although the performance gap with the benchmark method is generally acceptable, further research and optimization of the multivalued neural network are needed to enhance performance, especially in high SNR scenarios.
It is worth noting that existing DL techniques and parameterized approaches for 2D AoA and AoD estimation require reexecution of these methods when new target estimations are required. Recognizing this limitation, our upcoming research will focus on developing tracking mechanisms within both DL and parameterized methods. This intentional integration aims to simplify the update process for AoA and AoD estimation, eliminating the need for reestimation of sensor parameters, particularly in dynamic scenarios.
